Travel rules do not stay simple for long.

One trip is easy to reason about. A year of trips, passports and permissions is not.

Rules are scattered

Stay limits sit on separate official pages, each with its own wording and conditions.

Date arithmetic piles up

Rolling windows and calendar periods are easy to miscount once several trips overlap.

Plans affect each other

A new trip changes the room left for the next one. A delay can fix one stay and break another.

Rules change after you plan

An answer you worked out in March may not hold in June.

Bookmarks go stale

Screenshots and saved links do not tell you when they stopped being true.

Documents are an afterthought

A passport expiring before a return date is found late, not early.

What you get from it.

Room before you book

See how a planned stay interacts with represented stay limits and how much allowance remains, before money is spent.

The year, not isolated trips

A calendar and Year view put history and plans on one timeline.

Alternatives when it does not fit

Deterministic options such as shortening, delaying, a permission programme or supplying a missing input. They are kept separate from the regulatory result.

Sources stay attached

Each result names the represented rule, its source and when it was last verified.

Know when to re-check

Results say when they are stale, and whether your facts or the rules changed.

Document readiness

Note which documents a stay needs, which you hold and when they expire.

More than one traveller

Keep several travellers in one organisation and plan shared stays.

Where can I go next?

Try a hypothetical trip against the represented destinations. Nothing you search for is saved.

A budget estimate

Keep your own cost estimate per plan in one currency. CrossBorder does not look up prices or exchange rates.

Results you can trace.

CrossBorder does not hand you an opaque verdict. A result is produced by deterministic code from rules that have been entered with a source and reviewed. It is not produced by AI.

Represented rules, with sources

Each result names the rule it used, the source (title, publisher, last-checked date), whether the rule is confirmed or only likely, and its effective dates.

Versioned, with history

Rules are versioned. Every check is kept as history, so an earlier result is never overwritten by a later one.

Changes make results stale, on the record

When a represented rule changes the result of a stay you checked, CrossBorder re-evaluates that stay, keeps the earlier result and raises an alert describing what changed.

Unknown stays unknown

For a destination that is not represented, CrossBorder says so. Missing coverage is never shown as “allowed”.

Security and data handling →

What CrossBorder does not do.

CrossBorder helps you understand and monitor represented travel rules. It does not issue visas, guarantee entry, or replace advice or decisions from immigration authorities.

Not a visa service

It does not apply for, submit or track visas or other applications.

Not advice

It is not legal, immigration or tax advice. A “Ready” result means no conflict was found under the confirmed rules represented. It is not approval.

Not everywhere

Coverage is maintained by nCrypt and is not comprehensive. It can be missing or need re-checking.

Not a booking or data tool

It does not book travel, read documents or look up live prices. Documents are readiness notes: nothing is uploaded and no passport numbers are stored.

CrossBorder is in Pilot 0.

The planning, checking, monitoring and document-readiness workflow is live. Regulatory coverage is whatever has been entered and reviewed so far, and alerts appear in the app only, not by email or text. There is no billing yet.

Questions before you start

What does CrossBorder calculate?

For each planned stay it applies the represented rules for that destination and your passport and permission: per-entry limits, rolling windows, calendar periods and cool-off periods. The same inputs always give the same result.

Where do the rules come from?

They are entered by nCrypt with a supporting source and reviewed before they are used. Each result shows the rule, its source and when it was last verified.

What happens if a rule changes?

If the change alters the result of a stay you have checked, CrossBorder re-evaluates that stay, keeps the earlier result in history and raises an in-app alert. Results that no longer match your facts or the rules are marked stale until re-checked.

Does CrossBorder replace an immigration adviser?

No. It is a planning aid, not legal, immigration or tax advice, and no result is a guarantee. Check the official source before you rely on anything marked “Needs verification”.

Does it apply for visas?

No. It does not apply for, submit or track visas or any other application.

Is every country and programme supported?

No. Only destinations and programmes that have been entered and reviewed are represented. For anything else CrossBorder says it does not have enough represented information. That is not a sign that anything is allowed or forbidden.

Can I track more than one traveller?

Yes. An organisation can hold several travellers, and plans can include shared stays.

What information do I need to enter?

A traveller name, passport issuing country and expiry, any permissions held, and past and planned stays. Passport numbers, scans and uploads are not stored and should not be entered.
